# Percent-of-max, waves, and readiness in strength apps

Research date 2026-11-26. Scope: how top strength apps handle %1RM / training-max (TM) programming, wave templates, and pre-session readiness, and what a quiet local-first logger with a per-exercise progression rule seam should take from them.

Source quality is marked: **[P]** primary (app help center, vendor docs, study), **[S]** secondary (reviews, guides, forums), **[M]** from memory, not verified this session. Where a primary source did not state something, it says "not documented". Reddit and review-site complaints could not be fetched directly; the pitfalls section marks which items are sourced and which are inference.

## 1. Percent-of-1RM and training max

Liftosaur's `rm1` is **one value per exercise, shared across programs**, editable in Exercise Stats, and assignable from a script (`rm1 += 5lb`) [P].

### TM as a percentage of 1RM

- 5/3/1: TM is 90% of true 1RM in the original books. Later guidance allows 85%, and lower for beginners. The sources are secondary: [Lift Vault TM guide](https://liftvault.com/resources/531-training-max-definition-examples/) (80-85% a good rule of thumb), a StrongFirst forum comment that Wendler later advocated 85% [S]. Boostcamp states "90% of your true 1RM" [P].
- Strong Strong Friends defines TM behaviourally, not as a percentage: a weight you can lift for one confident rep on a bad day and two to three solid reps on a good day [P].
- Liftosaur's Juggernaut template is written directly in %1RM (54% at the start of the 10s wave) because "the percentages are already adjusted from the book's training-max figures" [P: [Liftosaur Juggernaut](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/juggernaut-method)]. Lift Vault's spreadsheet uses 60% of TM for the same week, which is the same load. **The base (1RM vs TM) must be explicit per program or numbers differ by about 10%** [S].

### Rounding

- Liftosaur: weights always round to what the user's configured plates and equipment can make; `increment(weight)` / `decrement(weight)` return the nearest achievable weight above or below [P].
- Hevy's warm-up calculator has a separate plate increment and dumbbell increment setting [P: [Hevy](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/warm-up-set-calculator/)].
- An open-source 5/3/1 tracker rounds prescriptions up to the nearest 5 lb [S]. Round-up vs nearest is a real choice: rounding up on a TM-based top set inflates the AMRAP standard.

### Failure handling

- 5/3/1: missing the **prescribed** reps (not the AMRAP) means the TM is too high. Guides say reset 10-15% (i.e. 3-4 cycles back) and rebuild; reset only the stalled lift [S: [Lift Vault](https://liftvault.com/resources/amrap-pr-set/), forum posts]. I could not reach Wendler's own wording; the exact numbers vary by source.
- 7th week protocol (5/3/1 Forever): run between blocks. Two forms: **deload** or **TM test**. Test form: work up to a set at 100% TM; about 3-5 solid reps confirms the TM, under 3 means lower it [S: [Lift Vault](https://liftvault.com/resources/7th-week-protocol/), page returned 403 to full fetch; summary from search]. Deload form details disagree between sources (40/50/60% x5 vs a ramp to a single at TM). Wendler moved the deload from every 4th week to roughly every 7th [S].
- Liftosaur's own 5/3/1-style example counts failures in a state variable and cuts the weight 15 lb after more than two misses, then resets the counter [S via search summary, [docs](https://www.liftosaur.com/docs/docs.html)].
- Juggernaut (Liftosaur port): "if this happens consistently, the starting 1RM was likely too high; reduce 5-10%" [P].

How Liftosaur represents the wave: read-only `week`, `day`, `dayInWeek` variables, plus **Sets Variations** where a "Variation Expression" returns a 1-based index (e.g. `day == 1 ? 1 : 2`) choosing among Sets x Reps schemes [P]. ### Liftosaur's model (the best reference for a rule seam)

- Program -> days -> exercises -> sets. A set has reps, weight, and AMRAP flag; each is a Liftoscript expression [P].
- **Per-exercise persistent state** (`state.weight`, `state.failures`, user-defined) survives between workouts. This is the whole memory of a rule.
- **Progress** is a function run at day finish. Built-ins: `lp(increase, increaseAttempts, currentAttempt, decrease, decreaseAttempts, currentDecreaseAttempt)` (linear, with attempt counters for both stall-hold and deload), `dp(increase, minReps, maxReps)` (double progression), `sum(repsThreshold, increase)` (total-reps trigger), and `custom()` (arbitrary script with declared state) [P: [liftoscript](https://www.liftosaur.com/doc/liftoscript)].
- Script inputs: `weights[n]`, `reps[n]`, `completedReps[n]`, `RPE[n]`, `completedRPE[n]`, `amraps[n]`, `week`, `day`, `rm1`, `bodyweight`. Script outputs: state changes, and `rm1` (shared) changes. The editor shows a "State Changes" preview (`weight: 50lb -> 55lb`) before you commit [P].
- Unit-aware numbers (`lb`/`kg` suffix) convert when the user switches units [P].
- Limit: in the planner, state variables cannot be used directly in reps, weight or timer fields, unlike classic Liftoscript [S].

Pattern: apps that **act** on readiness (Juggernaut AI, RP) use a handful of subjective items and change volume or load; wearables score but do not change the plan. Asking costs a tap per session and most users answer with a default.

### Evidence

- RPE vs fixed %1RM, 8-week matched DUP programs (n = 21): both gained strength and muscle thickness, no significant difference; authors say RPE-based may give a small 1RM advantage to most individuals, and %1RM is vulnerable to a bad test day [P: [Helms et al. 2018, Front Physiol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29628895/)].
- 2024 meta-analysis of 15 studies: autoregulated (RIR-RPE or velocity) vs percentage-based loading showed no significant 1RM or hypertrophy difference; velocity-loss thresholds of 25% or less gave more strength [S via search summary, [PMC review list](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11042849/)].
- Scoping review of meta-session autoregulation: subjective readiness measures often lacked evidence of validity, and 45% of included studies were poor quality [S via search summary: [PMC11042849](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11042849/)]. Another review states the Borg CR10 scale seemed unreliable in resistance training.
- Net: autoregulation is not shown to beat plain progression; readiness questionnaires are weakly validated. I did not find a trial of a Hooper-style wellness score driving daily load.

## 5. Notable ideas worth borrowing

- **Warm-ups generated from the working weight**: Hevy builds sets from an entered target weight, default percent/rep formula editable per set, "Reset to Default", add/remove buttons, three sets as the default, rounded to the user's plate and dumbbell increments [P]. For a %TM program the working weight is derived, so warm-ups derive from that.
- **Prompt, do not auto-apply, the TM bump** (Strong Strong Friends): a confirmation preserves trust and handles edge cases (bad AMRAP day). Liftosaur shows a before/after state diff first.
- **TM bump formula from AMRAP overshoot** (Juggernaut): `(reps - standard) x per-rep increment`, capped, never negative. Simple, explainable, one number.
- **Plate-aware rounding as a core service**, not per-feature (Liftosaur).
- **Shared per-exercise max across programs** (Liftosaur `rm1`) avoids re-entering the same bench max for each program.
- **Frozen vs derived max** (TrainHeroic): a tested or user-set max is locked; estimates only fill in when nothing is set.
- **Deload triggers**: fixed (the last week of each wave), or attempt counters (`lp` decrease attempts), or the TM-test result. Autoregulated triggers (RP) depend on feedback fields we would have to collect.
- **Joker sets / FSL**: derived sets keyed off the top set or the week's first set. FSL is a derived set group (n x reps at first-set percent), not a separate exercise.

## Implications for an app with a per-exercise rule seam (Double / Linear / Top+backoffs / Maintain), week phases (Build / Push / Test / Deload), e1RM tracking

### Data model

1. **Training max lives on the exercise, not the program.** One current TM per exercise (like Liftosaur's `rm1`), with a **history of TM changes** (value, date, reason: manual, AMRAP bump, test, reset, program id). Programs read it; a rule writes it. Per-program TMs invite stale duplicates.
2. **Store the base explicitly**: `tm` plus `tmPercentOfE1rm` (default 90, 85 allowed), and the percentages in a template expressed against TM. Do not mix 1RM-based and TM-based tables silently (the 54% vs 60% trap).
3. **Weeks are lookups, phases are labels.** A wave template = ordered list of weeks, each `{phase, sets: [{pct, reps, amrap}]}`. Phase (Build/Push/Test/Deload) selects both the lookup row and which rule actions are allowed (bump on Push, test on Test, never on Deload).
4. **Cycle index and wave index are program-instance state**, not exercise state. Rules need `week`, `cycleIndex`, `phase` as read-only inputs (Liftosaur exposes `week`, `day`, `dayInWeek`).
5. **Per-exercise rule state** (Liftosaur's `state.*`): failure/attempt counters, GZCL stage, last successful weight. A small typed record per rule kind, not a free-form bag (a repo rule bans `Record<string, unknown>`).
6. **Rule outputs are proposals**: `{newWeight?, newTm?, newStage?, reason}` shown as a diff, accepted on finish or next open. Keep reasons as data so history can say why a number changed.
7. **e1RM**: compute per set from weight, reps, and RPE when present, store the formula id with the result (or recompute from raw sets) so formula changes do not rewrite history. Use e1RM only to **suggest** an initial TM (`90% of e1RM`), never as the live prescription.
8. **Rounding** is a function of the user's plate set and unit, applied after all percentage maths, with one documented direction (nearest, with round-down on AMRAP standards).
9. **Readiness, if added, is a per-session optional record** (a few 1-5 items), applied as a **session-only** modifier (drop load or sets, or lower RPE target) and never written back to TM or rule state. This matches Juggernaut AI's "that workout only" behaviour and the weak evidence for validity.

### Pitfalls (sourced where marked, otherwise design inference)

- **Failure semantics differ by template**: "missed prescribed reps" vs "missed AMRAP standard" lead to different outcomes in 5/3/1 [S]. A single `failed` flag is not enough; the rule needs per-set results and which set was the AMRAP.
- **TMs that only go up** [S: The Plus Set] mean a bad cycle can never self-correct. Allow a user-approved reset and a TM-test phase.
- **TM not saving or silently changing** shows up in app reviews of 5/3/1 apps [S: [Five/Three/One reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1560266240/five-three-one-531-workouts/reviews)]. Make every TM change visible, reversible, and logged with a reason.
- **Round-up inflates AMRAP standards** and makes the first top set heavier than the percent says (inference from the round-up behaviour above).
- **Shared programs produce different numbers per user** when the e1RM formula differs [P: Liftosaur docs]. Pin the formula to the template or show it.
- **Advanced scripting is powerful but hard to learn** (Liftosaur ships a playground and state-change preview to compensate; its planner cannot use state in reps/weight) [S]. A fixed menu of rule kinds with editable parameters avoids this for most users.
- **Auto progression gated behind a paywall** (Boostcamp Pro) is a common complaint class; for a local-first app, ship rule execution free [S: Boostcamp pricing page; the complaint itself is inference].
- **Readiness prompts add friction and weak evidence**: keep optional, one screen, skippable, and do not gate the session.
- **Deload and test weeks must not trigger bumps**; a Test phase result (3-5 reps at 100% TM) should _propose_ keep / lower, not raise.
- **Unverified here**: GZCLP exact numbers, nSuns/Texas/Sheiko specifics, Strong/Hevy/JEFIT feature gaps, direct Reddit complaint threads, RP's real feedback decision table. Verify before building those.
